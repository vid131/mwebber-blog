September is coming fast, and if you have lingering summer goals, you better have a plan.

In our house, lingering isn't really in the cards anyway. Our to-do list is always stacked. This summer has been no different. We have a list of large projects and commitments we want to accomplish. They’re not all getting done, and we know that.

There’s a corner of our backyard near the shed that’s currently just dirt. We have visions of turning it into a real gathering space with outdoor furniture and a mounted outdoor TV. Inside, we’re taking over a corner fireplace project abandoned by a contractor, choosing to finish the work ourselves rather than chase down someone else willing to take on a small job.

And then there's the August cycling goal: 300 miles on the road together for the American Cancer Society.

On paper, adding 75 miles a week to a month already packed with hard labor and daily obligations sounds like bad planning. In practice, it forces a level of clarity that an open schedule never delivers.

**When you have open, unstructured hours, it's easy to deliberate, check off low-priority tasks, or get pulled into minor distractions.** But when your calendar is overloaded, you operate more like a student staring down a project due tomorrow morning.

You don't deliberate; you execute.

There’s a myth that productivity requires stretches of quiet, uninterrupted time. But often, the opposite is true. An overloaded schedule with hard boundaries creates an environment where distraction simply can't survive.

The summer is moving fast, the projects are real, and the mileage is waiting. Having a lot on your plate isn't the problem, it's the exact reason the work gets done.
